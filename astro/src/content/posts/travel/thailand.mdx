## Disclaimers:

1) Not everything written here has got any particular significance to the places I visited in Thailand as such. Some are just my experiences, which could have happened to me elsewhere too. But that it has happened here, is why it is here.

2) At places, I may be seen generalizing a few things, so don't fall into my ignorant, lazy conclusions. I may be right, but I might also be wrong. How much can a person, who has stayed just for a week in a land, speak of it, with any degree of certainty!

That said, let me begin.

## Day 5, Krabi

Mornings are emphatically empty in Thailand (First of my many generalizations, I suppose). I get out and hit the street at 7 in the morning. What next? A cup of tea? No. I  can't find. With that the morning is already incomplete.

I saunter along the empty street and head into an open cafe or restaurant of sorts. It's all empty: the chairs, the counter, the snooker table and everything else. It was open all night I suppose and hence will sleep the morning, and perhaps all the way to the evening. I look around, pull a chair and sit, overlooking the sea.

The waves are hitting the shore, and there is breeze accompanying it. I'm staring at the horizon or perhaps at the heart of the sea. I'm trying to make this a perfect morning. Bu it isn't one, and like many other mornings, this is just another morning, not the perfect one that we want it to be. I tell to myself - Stop romanticizing; accept the imperfection.

Lying on the shore is a plastic bottle. On the entire stretch, I can only see one. But one is no less. With every attempt, waves are closing in inch by inch. And slowly they come in and finally consume the trash. Never have I seen poison being consumed so gracefully, and eloquently. 

The poison is slow. It will one day kill the sea, and us too. No one is to be spared.

After a while, I leave the place and walk along the stretch of the street. I reach the interlocked pathway with a handful of seats, and trees planted equidistantly. Overnight rain has resulted in small puddles of water being formed in patches. And the little sparrows are hovering around these patches and hopping across the boughs of the trees.

One sparrow among them, lands at the puddle and rubs its feathers in the water. It stops for a while and continues to rub vehemently. It repeats and then flies off to a drier place and ruffles its feathers so as to brush off the water. This cycle of wetting its feathers first and drying it up next is repeated several times.

And for some time, I was lost in its world. It was such a wonderful sight that it made my morning!

## Day 7, Bangkok

Hot sunny day, 10 am in the morning. I'm waiting at the bus stop for the airport bus. Apart from the cars and bikes on the road, nothing much is happening. Almost all the shops are closed, and there are hardly a few people walking on the street.

Next to the bus stop, a small shop with some cookies, chocolates and cool drinks, is open. I enter the shop to get myself a drink. An old man is sitting inside, with his dog. He doesn't speak a word; gives me the drink that I asked for. 

Silence had made its home here.

A long street sans people, empty shops, a pathway full of trees, a bus stop, hot sun, pleasant shade, a small shop and an old man with his dog; It is a beautiful image. Try picturing it. I can’t do justice to it with my words. If I was good at painting, I would have given it a try. But I'm not. So, I'm labouring and writing an image, not capturing it, just recording, as a reference.

But, why? For some strange reason, I cannot get enough of this image. So, I had to...

## Day 6, Tiger caves

We had to leave by 5 to catch the flight to Bangkok. Tiger caves was our last destination for the day. We still had close to 3 hours to spend at the place, and there was nothing much to do, apart from a summit to climb, and that only if we wanted to, on top of which Buddha stood.

No one seemed interested. Even I, very hesitantly started to climb. It had 1260 stairs, and the ascent was very vertical in nature. After climbing some 200 odd steps, I said to myself I'm completing this.

By the time I reached the top, I was drenched in sweat. I like it when I sweat. I get a strange sort of pleasure out of it, especially when the whole of my shirt is etched to my skin with sweat. So, what is so special about sweating profusely?

When you sweat from head to toe, the whole of your body is breathing, the whole of it is living. Every cell is inhaling and exhaling, celebrating its life. All it needs is an opportunity to express itself. And all you need to do is to make up your mind, for your body to live its life. So, when you run your body just like any other machine, and it then sweats, I see it as a reward for the unification of body and mind. The roots of my pleasure lie here, I suppose.



Tiger caves summit


Enough said about mind and body, let me touch base upon a bit of Thailand I saw.

Ours was mainly an Island tour, from Phuket to James Bond island to Phi Phi to Krabi. Bangkok was only our last pit stop. Since I had to leave early, I got little time in Bangkok. Needless to say, the maximum time I spent in Bangkok was at the airport!

All the places we visited were kept extremely clean, all the roads that we travelled across were wide enough and were very well maintained. Everything around looked lush green, couple of showers also helped the cause. The sea shores and the beaches that we visited didn't leave us with any room to complain with regards to cleanliness. 

All this in itself was very refreshing!



Ko Poda island


As we were hopping from one island to the other, most of our time was spent either in ferries or long tail boats. The rest of the time we were either floating on the sea waters with our life jackets or snorkelling and peeping into the life under water or sauntering around the beaches.



James Bond island

Snorkelling was something very new to me and one which I enjoyed. The sea water at places was crystal clear and we could see through the water: the fishes, urchins and corals. At night, we also got a peek into the shining plankton.

The colors beneath the water are different to that of the ones above it!

Not once on our tour, we had to wait for the taxi or the ferry or the boat. All that was booked was on time. This level of promptness took us by a certain degree of shock. Thailand takes its tourism very seriously. The travel agencies, the taxi drivers, the boatmen are very prompt, from what we experienced.

Let me stop here for a moment.

What is extremely clean or very prompt or very honest? That what is clean is clean, promptness is promptness, honesty is honesty and truth is truth. When you add an adjective to these basic qualities, something is intrinsically wrong. Isn't it?

We are so much used to uncleanliness that when we see something clean, we jump off the gun to say extremely clean. When a taxi arrives on time, to which we are not used to, we label it as very prompt, when it is just being prompt. We face up to so many lies around that when we happen to hear the truth, we brand it very truthful, when in essence there is only either truth or false! Come to think of it as I leave it here.

Off to Phi Phi island. Meet the boatman.

It's off season, so the boatman is pretty relaxed and has got nothing much to do, and he keeps himself busy by checking out the girls visiting the beach. When asked if he goes out of Phi Phi anytime, only once or twice a year, he says, lethargically. Phi Phi is beautiful, and all the beautiful ladies of the world come to Phi Phi, so why should I want to go out, he asks, as he bursts into a laughter. 

Looking at the boatman, one is convinced that he is happy with where he is. He wears that expression of - "God couldn't have been more kind to me". Ahh...the Life in the islands...!

Coming to the night life in Thailand...

In Phuket, the most happening street at night, is the Bangla street. It is a sin to enter the street without a beer in hand, and we had already committed it. And if that wasn't enough we took no shame in it. Laughing and joking at ourselves we walk along. In the whole of that street, we were the complete miss fits. But little did it matter to us.

Like shirts put on hangers in a shopping mall, girls line up on top of the table, making their moves. The girls, boys and lady boys throng the place, making all sorts of gestures and noise. It is more than just a party street, and pretty much everything you imagine to happen, happens here!

We walked the entire stretch of the street and as if that didn't make us feel stupid enough, we took the same route back, just to see how it feels like to be more stupid.

On our way back, we collected some litchis and rambutans (no whiskies or rums, just some fruits), and had them at our rooms, just to make such a night worse if it already wasn't! But I must say, the litchis were really great, and all agreed on it.

Look, we're passing a judgement on the taste of a litchi fruit after returning from Bangla street. By now you must have concluded that we are indeed a special bunch of species! And, I firmly stand with you.

Earlier in the evening, we visited a couple of Buddhist temples, and on the way back I sat next to the tuk tuk driver. I knew nothing of Thai and he knew very little English. So, our conversations had minimal words, and between those words was a long pause.

Apart from driving his auto, he was also a part time trainer of Thai Boxing, so he himself trained his 4-year-old kid. He then went on to show me a couple of video clips of him training his kid.

To a random tourist guy, a father is showing a video of his kid boxing - in that very moment, with a stranger, I felt the warmth of togetherness. We shared a space common to both.

It doesn't matter where we live, what language we speak, as long as we understand that we all live under the same umbrella of humanity and brotherhood.
