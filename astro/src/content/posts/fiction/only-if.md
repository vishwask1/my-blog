It was just another morning - the alarm rang; the sun was out; the milkman, as per his schedule, had placed the packet of milk in the bag left hanging on the door; the newspaper, as expectedly, had already been delivered to the doorstep, and as I stepped out to fetch them, I looked at the the kid next door, in his uniform, waiting for the lift to arrive, dismissive in his glare towards me, as if he had seen a species which stretched beyond his imaginations, and made a face, which brought me slight shame, one I couldn't immediately hide. But I stood there, staring at my morning, which screamed aloud - You're late. Run. 

Everyone and everything around, had performed their roles as prescribed, and left me with no option but accept my fate.

But, only if - my phone had run out of battery or the dark clouds had masked the sun from the vicinity of this earth, or the milkman had a funeral to attend, or the newspaper boy had a flat tyre, or the kid was late to school. Only if, I could find an excuse to break out of my routine and step out - to collect the newspaper or buy a packet of milk or under the pretence of having had my morning even slightly disturbed, head to the tea shop by the end of the street and find a corner for myself, to sip a cup of tea and watch - things unfold in front of me; how a swarm of flies, miraculously, happen to find themselves, in the same very spot, at the same very time of the day, just like how they did yesterday or the day before.

If I could sit and watch - the lady, all dressed up, agonizingly wait for her cab, worried, equally, by the gaze of the men around her and the rays of the sun beating down on her face, intensity of both, growing by the minute; a father, who had come to drop his kids, curse the school bus which hadn't arrived on time, act restless, for he couldn't wait for his day to start, only so that he could end it on his own terms before time caught up on him; the gentleman, adjust his tie, in the face of an unforgiving morning, glance at his watch, one too many times than one normally would, for he has an appointment to keep, the chances of which are slipping just as the sweat drips down his face; a flock of people, on their wheels, wait for the signal to turn green, but meanwhile, as if from a baraat band set in a wedding procession, blow horns like trumpets, jittery and itching to move ahead of one another, only to save a few irrelevant seconds or at most minutes or nothing at all, and to find themselves repeat the same at the next signal they are to end up; a bunch of retired or about to retire uncles, just having returned from an hour of badminton game, now sip coffee and burst their lungs out in laughter, as if all of the street's happiness concentrated on this very select few, who are sure though, of the fact that their best part of the day all but over and have a long day ahead at their disposal to do nothing but wait for the next morning to come.

If I could break my routine, and watch - humanity meander along helplessly, in the manner that it does, as if controlled by a power from above, that it either, doesn't see but believes in and follows, or is aware of its cunning vagaries but can't help but follow - and then realize I wouldn't be a part of this tragic circus, at least for the day, would give me a sense of voyeuristic pleasure. As I would sip my tea to the full and leave the cup to dry with my thoughts, I would wonder how could one not be happy at another man's misery. These useless pawns, morons, and what not...

But, not surprisingly, none of it happened, and just like y 'day, I was left with little option but to accept the morning as it came. And, as I was brooding over those thoughts, I didn't realize the kid had left long back even though his glare remained, but then I heard a scream from inside, demanding my presence, and I rushed back.

So, in my diary, I wrote, of the morning where nothing happened - "Today I woke up to a morning same as y 'day, and I couldn't say If I was living in the present or the past. And sadly, there is no nicer way to say it..."
